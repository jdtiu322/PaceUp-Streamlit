# Race Day

## Nothing New

Do not try new shoes, socks, gels, caffeine, breakfast, or warmup routines on race day. Race day should repeat what already worked in training.

If a runner has not practiced a product or routine, keep the race plan simple and familiar.

## Pre-Race Timing

Arrive early enough to use the bathroom, check gear, warm up, and reach the start without rushing. Rushing before the start can raise stress and lead to poor pacing.

For morning races, eat a familiar breakfast that has worked before training runs. Keep the meal simple and easy to digest.

## Warmup

For a 5K or 10K, warm up with 10 to 20 minutes of easy jogging, light drills, and 3 to 5 relaxed strides. The goal is to feel awake, not tired.

For a half marathon or marathon, use a shorter warmup unless the runner is experienced. Save energy for the race and rely on the first miles to settle in.

## Pacing

Most runners race better with a controlled start. The first part of the race should feel slightly easier than the final part.

If the runner starts too fast, advise them to relax, settle into goal effort, and avoid panic. One fast split does not ruin a race if they correct early.

## 5K Race Strategy

A 5K should start controlled but assertive. The first kilometer should not feel like a sprint. The middle section requires focus, and the final kilometer is where the runner can press harder.

If the runner is nervous, suggest using the first 3 minutes to settle breathing and rhythm before judging the pace.

## 10K Race Strategy

A 10K requires patience. The first half should feel controlled and repeatable. If the runner feels strong after halfway, they can gradually increase effort.

Going out too fast in a 10K often causes a large slowdown late, so controlled early pacing matters.

## Half Marathon Strategy

A half marathon should begin at a sustainable rhythm. The runner should avoid racing the first 5K too hard. The key section is often from 10K to 18K, where focus and fueling matter.

If the runner has a goal pace, use the first few miles to lock into rhythm rather than chasing every small GPS fluctuation.

## Marathon Strategy

The marathon rewards restraint. The first half should feel controlled. If the runner is working hard early, they are likely running too fast.

Fueling should start early, before the runner feels depleted. Waiting until energy drops is usually too late.

## Bad Weather

In heat or humidity, slow the pace and prioritize effort. In strong wind, tuck into groups when possible and avoid surging too hard into headwinds.

Weather-adjusted pacing is smart racing, not failure.

## Post-Race

After finishing, walk for a few minutes, hydrate, eat something familiar, and change into dry clothes if needed. Hard races often require several easy days before normal training resumes.
