# Nutrition

## Daily Fueling

Runners need enough total energy to support training, recovery, mood, and health. Under-fueling can show up as fatigue, poor sleep, irritability, frequent soreness, stalled progress, or recurring injury.

Meals should generally include carbohydrates for training energy, protein for repair, fats for overall health, and colorful foods for micronutrients.

## Before A Run

For easy short runs, some runners are comfortable with a light snack or no snack. For harder or longer runs, eating beforehand often improves performance.

A pre-run snack should be familiar and easy to digest. Good simple options include toast, banana, oatmeal, rice, or another carbohydrate the runner already tolerates.

## During Long Runs

For runs longer than about 75 to 90 minutes, practice taking carbohydrates during the run. The exact amount depends on the runner, duration, intensity, and tolerance.

Fueling should be practiced in training before race day. The gut can be trained, but new fueling strategies should not debut during a race.

## After A Run

After hard or long runs, eat a meal or snack with carbohydrates and protein. Carbohydrates help restore energy, and protein supports muscle repair.

If the next workout is within 24 hours, recovery nutrition becomes more important.

## Hydration

Hydration needs change with heat, humidity, sweat rate, and run duration. Pale yellow urine can be a rough sign of normal hydration, but it is not a perfect measure.

For long runs or hot conditions, consider electrolytes, especially for runners who sweat heavily or notice salt marks on clothing.

## Race Day Fueling

Race day fueling should copy what worked in training. Do not introduce new gels, drinks, caffeine amounts, or breakfast foods on race morning.

For longer races, start fueling before energy drops. Waiting until the runner feels depleted often makes it harder to recover.

## Caffeine

Caffeine can help some runners feel more alert, but it can also cause jitters or stomach issues. Use only amounts that have been tested before important runs.

If the runner does not normally use caffeine, do not suggest starting with a large dose on race day.

## Sensitive Stomach

If a runner has stomach issues, simplify the pre-run meal, avoid unfamiliar foods, reduce fat and fiber close to the run, and test changes during training.

Persistent digestive problems should be discussed with a qualified professional.
