# Hydration Evidence

## Hydration plans should be individualized

Evidence summary:
The American College of Sports Medicine position stand on exercise and fluid replacement emphasizes starting exercise in a normal hydration state and personalizing fluid replacement because sweat rate and sweat sodium loss vary widely between individuals.

Practical PaceUp use:
Do not give a single fluid number for every runner. Ask about duration, weather, sweat rate, salt marks, stomach tolerance, and access to fluids. For long races, encourage testing the plan in training.

Citation:
- Title: Exercise and Fluid Replacement
- Authors: Michael N. Sawka, Louise M. Burke, E. Randy Eichner, Ronald J. Maughan, Scott J. Montain, Nina S. Stachenfeld

## Large fluid losses can impair endurance performance

Evidence summary:
A comprehensive physiology review reports that dehydration can impair endurance performance, with commonly discussed impairment thresholds around body mass losses of about 2 percent in many endurance contexts. The exact impact depends on environment, exercise mode, intensity, and the type of fluid loss.

Practical PaceUp use:
For long or hot runs, advise runners to limit large dehydration rather than chasing exact body weight replacement every time. Encourage practical checks such as body mass change in training, thirst, urine color, and symptoms.

Citation:
- Title: Dehydration: physiology, assessment, and performance effects
- Authors: Samuel N. Cheuvront, Robert W. Kenefick
- Year: 2014
- Source type: Review
- DOI: 10.1002/cphy.c130017
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1002/cphy.c130017

## Avoid both dehydration and overdrinking

Evidence summary:
The ACSM fluid replacement position stand focuses on preventing excessive dehydration and excessive electrolyte disturbance. A runner's safest plan is usually personalized drinking that accounts for sweat rate, conditions, duration, and sodium needs rather than forcing fluid beyond tolerance.

Practical PaceUp use:
Do not tell runners to drink as much as possible. For long races, recommend a practiced drinking plan that prevents major fluid deficit while avoiding overdrinking, especially in slower marathoners or very long events.

## Heat and humidity should shift advice from pace to effort

Evidence summary:
Hydration and thermoregulation research shows that fluid balance, heat stress, and cardiovascular strain interact during endurance exercise. Hot and humid conditions raise the cost of maintaining a given pace, so performance targets often need adjustment.

Practical PaceUp use:
In heat or humidity, advise runners to slow pace targets, use effort and heart rate cautiously, consider earlier fluids, and reduce workout ambition if symptoms appear.

Citation:
- Title: Physiological adjustments to hypohydration: Impact on thermoregulation
- Authors: Robert W. Kenefick, Samuel N. Cheuvront
- Year: 2016
- Source type: Review
- DOI: 10.1016/j.autneu.2016.02.003
