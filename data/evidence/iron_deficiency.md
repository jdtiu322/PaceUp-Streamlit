# Iron Deficiency Evidence

## Female endurance athletes can have a high prevalence of low iron stores

Evidence summary:
Recent observational evidence in highly trained female endurance athletes found a high prevalence of sub-optimal iron status. This supports iron screening as a real practical issue in endurance sport rather than a niche concern.

Practical PaceUp use:
If a runner reports unexplained fatigue, poor recovery, heavy menstrual losses, or a history of low ferritin, PaceUp should normalize screening rather than guessing. Advice should point runners toward testing, not immediate supplementing without context.

Citation:
- Title: High Prevalence of Iron Deficiency Exhibited in Internationally Competitive, Non-Professional Female Endurance Athletes-A Case Study
- Authors: Stacy T. Sims, Kelsi Mackay, Alana Leabeater, Anthea Clarke, Katherine Schofield, Matthew Driller
- Year: 2022
- Source type: Observational case study
- DOI: 10.3390/ijerph192416606
- PMID: 36554486
- URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph192416606

## Low energy availability can worsen iron-related stress in endurance runners

Evidence summary:
Experimental evidence in male long-distance runners found that short-term low energy availability during endurance training altered iron metabolism. This supports the idea that fueling problems and iron issues can overlap.

Practical PaceUp use:
When fatigue and low iron concerns appear together, PaceUp should consider whether the runner is simply under-fueling overall. This is especially relevant during high-volume training, weight-loss attempts, or restrictive eating patterns.

Citation:
- Title: Effect of low energy availability during three consecutive days of endurance training on iron metabolism in male long distance runners
- Authors: Aya Ishibashi, Chihiro Kojima, Yoko Tanabe, Kaito Iwayama, Kazushige Goto, Hideyuki Takahashi, and colleagues
- Year: 2020
- Source type: Experimental study
- DOI: 10.14814/phy2.14494
- PMID: 32597030
- URL: https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.14494

## Iron supplementation decisions should be based on testing and context

Evidence summary:
Review guidance on iron supplementation in athletes emphasizes that endurance athletes can be at increased risk of deficiency, but supplementation decisions still require interpretation of iron status rather than blanket treatment.

Practical PaceUp use:
PaceUp should not prescribe iron doses. The safer coaching role is to flag risk patterns, encourage appropriate blood work, and remind runners that both deficiency and unnecessary supplementation carry downsides.

Citation:
- Title: Iron supplementation in athletes. Current recommendations
- Authors: P. Nielsen, D. Nachtigall
- Year: 1998
- Source type: Review
