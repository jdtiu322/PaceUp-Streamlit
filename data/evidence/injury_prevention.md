# Injury Prevention Evidence

## Novice runners have a higher injury rate than recreational runners

Evidence summary:
A systematic review and meta-analysis reported higher running-related injury incidence in novice runners than in recreational runners when expressed per 1000 hours of running. This supports conservative progression for beginners.

Practical PaceUp use:
When a beginner asks for a plan, favor run-walk progression, easy effort, rest days, and gradual increases. Avoid giving aggressive speed work early unless the runner already has a base.

Citation:
- Title: Incidence of Running-Related Injuries Per 1000 h of Running in Different Types of Runners: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
- Authors: Solvej Videbaek, Andreas Moeballe Bueno, Rasmus Oestergaard Nielsen, Sten Rasmussen
- Year: 2015
- Source type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-015-0333-8
- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-015-0333-8

## Running injury risk is multifactorial

Evidence summary:
Systematic review evidence suggests that running-related injuries are influenced by a mix of training characteristics, health and lifestyle factors, morphology, biomechanics, and prior injury history. The evidence base is heterogeneous, so single-factor explanations should be avoided.

Practical PaceUp use:
If a runner reports repeated injuries, do not blame only shoes, cadence, or mileage. Review recent training changes, recovery, sleep, strength, terrain, prior injuries, pain behavior, and weekly load.

Citation:
- Title: Risk factors for running-related injuries: An umbrella systematic review
- Authors: Clara Knierim Correia, Jean Marlon Machado, Fabio Hech Dominski, Marcelo Peduzzi de Castro, Heiliane de Brito Fontana, Caroline Ruschel
- Year: 2024
- Source type: Umbrella systematic review
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jshs.2024.04.011
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38697289/

## Sudden training-load changes may increase risk, but evidence is not simple

Evidence summary:
A systematic review on changes in training load found limited eligible evidence. Several included studies associated recent increases in distance, velocity, or frequency with higher injury risk, but the review cautioned that the compiled evidence was not strong enough for a single precise rule.

Practical PaceUp use:
Use conservative progression as a coaching default, but avoid pretending that a universal rule such as "10 percent per week" is guaranteed. Adjust progression based on runner history, symptoms, recovery, and training age.

Citation:
- Title: Is there evidence for an association between changes in training load and running-related injuries? A systematic review
- Authors: Camma Damsted, Simone Glad, Rasmus Oestergaard Nielsen, Henrik Sorensen, Laurent Malisoux
- Year: 2018
- Source type: Systematic review
- PMID: 30534459
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30534459/

## Training-error evidence is mixed, so advice should stay cautious

Evidence summary:
A systematic review of training errors and running-related injuries found conflicting results across studies examining volume, duration, frequency, and intensity. The methodological quality of included studies was limited.

Practical PaceUp use:
When discussing injury prevention, recommend cautious load management and monitoring rather than claiming one training variable always causes injury. Encourage runners to respond early to pain and fatigue.

Citation:
- Title: Training errors and running related injuries: a systematic review
- Authors: Rasmus Oestergaard Nielsen, Ida Buist, Henrik Sorensen, Martin Lind, Sten Rasmussen
- Year: 2012
- Source type: Systematic review
- PMID: 22389869
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22389869/

## Gait retraining can change loading variables, but injury prevention claims should be careful

Evidence summary:
Systematic review evidence suggests gait retraining can change running mechanics such as step rate and loading variables. However, direct evidence that these changes prevent injuries is still limited.

Practical PaceUp use:
Do not prescribe cadence changes to every runner. Consider gait cues only when there is a clear reason, and keep changes small and gradual.

Citation:
- Title: The Effectiveness of Gait Retraining on Running Kinematics, Kinetics, Performance, Pain, and Injury in Distance Runners: A Systematic Review With Meta-analysis
- Authors: Eoin Doyle, Tim L. A. Doyle, Jason Bonacci, Joel T. Fuller
- Year: 2022
- Source type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.2519/jospt.2022.10585
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35128941/
