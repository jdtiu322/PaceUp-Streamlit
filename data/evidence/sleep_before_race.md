# Sleep Before Race Evidence

## Poor sleep before competition is common, especially in higher-stakes settings

Evidence summary:
Observational evidence suggests that many athletes report poor sleep quality before competition, with higher-risk groups including individual-sport and higher-level competitors. This means pre-race sleep problems are common enough that they should be normalized rather than treated as instant failure.

Practical PaceUp use:
If a runner is anxious because they slept badly the night before a race, avoid catastrophic language. Reinforce that nerves and imperfect sleep are common, and focus them on pacing, fueling, and staying calm instead of chasing perfection at the last minute.

Citation:
- Title: Prevalence of poor sleep quality in athletes before competition
- Authors: Alexandro Andrade, Guilherme Guimarães Bevilacqua, Pedro De Orleans Casagrande, Ricardo Brandt, Danilo Coimbra
- Year: 2021
- Source type: Observational study
- DOI: 10.1080/00913847.2020.1784688
- PMID: 32571135
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/00913847.2020.1784688

## Improving sleep across the training week is more reliable than panicking about one night

Evidence summary:
Systematic review evidence on sleep extension in athletes suggests that increasing sleep opportunity can support sports performance. The practical implication is that cumulative sleep habits often matter more than a desperate single-night fix.

Practical PaceUp use:
PaceUp should encourage runners to protect sleep in the days leading into key races rather than obsess over one pre-race night. Recommend familiar routines, earlier wind-down, and reduced late-night stimulation during race week.

Citation:
- Title: Sleep extension in athletes: what we know so far - A systematic review
- Authors: Aldo Coelho Silva, Andressa Silva, Ben J. Edwards, David Tod, Marco Túlio de Mello, and colleagues
- Year: 2021
- Source type: Systematic review
- DOI: 10.1016/j.sleep.2020.11.028
- PMID: 33352457
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleep.2020.11.028
