# Heat Acclimation Evidence

## Heat acclimation improves tolerance to hot racing conditions

Evidence summary:
Systematic review and meta-analytic evidence supports that repeated heat acclimation produces meaningful thermoregulatory adaptations and that some benefits can be retained and regained with re-induction. For endurance athletes, the practical outcome is usually lower physiological strain and better tolerance when training or racing in hot conditions.

Practical PaceUp use:
If a runner has a goal race in hot or humid weather, advise a structured acclimation block rather than relying on race-week guesswork. Emphasize several repeated exposures, gradual progression, and an effort-based adjustment during the adaptation period.

Citation:
- Title: Heat Acclimation Decay and Re-Induction: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
- Authors: Hein A. M. D. Daanen, Sebastien Racinais, Julien D. Périard
- Year: 2018
- Source type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-017-0808-x
- PMID: 29129022
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0808-x

## Passive heat strategies can be a practical alternative when full heat camps are unrealistic

Evidence summary:
Review evidence on passive heating strategies suggests that post-exercise hot water immersion, sauna use, and other passive exposures may provide useful heat acclimation support when formal training in hot environments is difficult to schedule.

Practical PaceUp use:
For runners who cannot safely or consistently train in the heat, suggest passive heat exposure only if they already tolerate training well and can recover appropriately. Keep the message practical: passive strategies support acclimation, but they do not replace sensible pacing, hydration, and symptom awareness.

Citation:
- Title: Passive Heating: Reviewing Practical Heat Acclimation Strategies for Endurance Athletes
- Authors: Storme L. Heathcote, Peter Hassmén, Shi Zhou, Christopher J. Stevens
- Year: 2018
- Source type: Review
- DOI: 10.3389/fphys.2018.01851
- PMID: 30618849
- URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2018.01851

## Cooling can help race-day performance, but it is not a substitute for acclimation

Evidence summary:
Systematic review evidence indicates that pre-cooling can improve endurance exercise performance in the heat, likely by lowering thermal strain before exercise begins. However, cooling is a race-day aid rather than a full replacement for acclimation.

Practical PaceUp use:
For hot events, PaceUp can suggest simple cooling tactics such as shade, cold fluids, or cooling garments before the start, while still prioritizing acclimation and conservative pacing.

Citation:
- Title: Pre-cooling for endurance exercise performance in the heat: a systematic review
- Authors: Paul R. Jones, Christian Barton, Dylan Morrissey, Nicola Maffulli, Stephanie Hemmings
- Year: 2012
- Source type: Systematic review
- DOI: 10.1186/1741-7015-10-166
- PMID: 23249542
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1186/1741-7015-10-166
