# Shoe Rotation And Gear Evidence

## Footwear comfort is a meaningful performance consideration

Evidence summary:
Systematic review and meta-analytic evidence suggests that more comfortable footwear is associated with improved running economy. This supports a practical, runner-centered approach to shoe choice rather than assuming that every performance claim comes from stiffness, plate shape, or branding alone.

Practical PaceUp use:
When a runner asks which shoe to race or train in, give comfort and confidence real weight. If two options are both appropriate, the more comfortable and familiar option is often the better recommendation.

Citation:
- Title: Increased footwear comfort is associated with improved running economy - a systematic review and meta-analysis
- Authors: K. Van Alsenoy, M. L. van der Linden, O. Girard, D. Santos
- Year: 2023
- Source type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.1080/17461391.2021.1998642
- PMID: 34726119
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34726119/

## Shoe features may affect injury risk in some runners, but not uniformly

Evidence summary:
A randomized controlled trial found lower overall injury risk in runners assigned motion control shoes, with the clearest benefit appearing in runners with pronated feet. This supports more individualized footwear thinking rather than one universal shoe rule.

Practical PaceUp use:
Do not promise that one shoe category prevents injury for everyone. Use footwear suggestions as a personalized experiment, especially when a runner has a clear movement pattern, comfort preference, or injury history.

Citation:
- Title: Injury risk in runners using standard or motion control shoes: a randomised controlled trial with participant and assessor blinding
- Authors: Laurent Malisoux, Nicolas Chambon, Nicolas Delattre, Nils Gueguen, Axel Urhausen, Daniel Theisen
- Year: 2016
- Source type: Randomized controlled trial
- DOI: 10.1136/bjsports-2015-095031
- PMID: 26746907
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26746907/

## Advanced race shoes can improve economy, but they still need testing in training

Evidence summary:
Controlled evidence indicates that carbon plate shoes can improve metabolic cost and performance measures in recreational runners, but changes in footwear geometry and stiffness can also alter tissue loading and tolerance.

Practical PaceUp use:
PaceUp should not recommend a brand-new race shoe on race day. Even if a shoe is potentially faster, runners should test it in training first to check comfort, calf and foot response, and fueling logistics.

Citation:
- Title: Carbon Plate Shoes Improve Metabolic Power and Performance in Recreational Runners
- Authors: Anders Nielsen, Jesper Franch, Christian Heyde, Mark de Zee, Uwe Kersting, Ryan Godsk Larsen
- Year: 2022
- Source type: Randomized cross-over study
- DOI: 10.1055/a-1776-7986
- PMID: 35523201
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35523201/
