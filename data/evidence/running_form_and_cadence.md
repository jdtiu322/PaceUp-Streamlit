# Running Form And Cadence Evidence

## Small cadence increases can reduce lower-limb loading

Evidence summary:
Biomechanical evidence shows that modest step-rate increases, often around 5 to 10 percent above preferred cadence, can reduce loading at the knee and hip during running. This does not mean every runner needs the same cadence target, but it supports cadence as a practical lever in some injury-related cases.

Practical PaceUp use:
Avoid telling runners there is one ideal cadence for everyone. Use cadence changes as a tool when the goal is to reduce overstriding or lower loading, and keep changes small enough to feel sustainable.

Citation:
- Title: Effects of step rate manipulation on joint mechanics during running
- Authors: Bryan C. Heiderscheit, Elizabeth S. Chumanov, Max P. Michalski, Christa M. Wille, Michael B. Ryan
- Year: 2011
- Source type: Biomechanics study
- DOI: 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181ebedf4
- PMID: 20581720
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20581720/

## Gait retraining can change mechanics, but performance gains are not guaranteed

Evidence summary:
Systematic review and meta-analytic evidence suggests gait retraining can change running kinematics and kinetics, and step-rate interventions can reduce some loading variables. However, evidence for improved running economy or outright performance enhancement is less consistent.

Practical PaceUp use:
Do not oversell form tweaks as speed hacks. Position gait retraining as a targeted intervention for specific problems such as pain, loading, or overstriding, and keep the runner's main training goals in view.

Citation:
- Title: The Effectiveness of Gait Retraining on Running Kinematics, Kinetics, Performance, Pain, and Injury in Distance Runners: A Systematic Review With Meta-analysis
- Authors: Eoin Doyle, Tim L. A. Doyle, Jason Bonacci, Joel T. Fuller
- Year: 2022
- Source type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.2519/jospt.2022.10585
- PMID: 35128941
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35128941/
