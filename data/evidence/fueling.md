# Fueling Evidence

## Carbohydrate intake during longer hard exercise supports endurance performance

Evidence summary:
Sports nutrition position stands support carbohydrate intake during extended moderate to high intensity exercise, especially when sessions last longer than about 60 to 70 minutes. A commonly recommended range is about 30 to 60 grams of carbohydrate per hour, adjusted for duration, intensity, tolerance, and the runner's gut training.

Practical PaceUp use:
For long runs, races, or hard sessions longer than about 75 to 90 minutes, suggest practicing carbohydrate intake during training. Start conservatively if the runner is new to fueling and increase only as tolerated.

Citation:
- Title: International society of sports nutrition position stand: nutrient timing
- Authors: Chad M. Kerksick, Shawn Arent, Brad J. Schoenfeld, Jeffrey R. Stout, Bill Campbell, and colleagues
- Year: 2017
- Source type: Position stand
- DOI: 10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4

## Nutrition strategies should be individualized to the athlete and event

Evidence summary:
The joint position statement from the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, Dietitians of Canada, and the American College of Sports Medicine states that well-chosen nutrition strategies can support performance and recovery. It emphasizes tailoring food, fluid, and supplement choices to training status, event demands, and individual tolerance.

Practical PaceUp use:
Avoid one-size-fits-all fueling. Ask about race distance, session duration, stomach tolerance, prior gel use, and meal timing when giving detailed fueling advice.

Citation:
- Title: Position of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, Dietitians of Canada, and the American College of Sports Medicine: Nutrition and Athletic Performance
- Authors: D. Travis Thomas, Kelly Anne Erdman, Louise M. Burke
- Year: 2016
- Source type: Joint position statement
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006

## Acute carbohydrate feeding can improve endurance performance, but context matters

Evidence summary:
A systematic review and meta-analysis of carbohydrate intake during endurance exercise found an overall performance benefit compared with placebo or control conditions. Effects varied by test type, with larger effects generally seen in time-to-exhaustion protocols than in time trials.

Practical PaceUp use:
For race-specific advice, frame carbs as a tool that helps preserve effort over longer or harder work, not as magic. Encourage practice before race day and use the runner's tolerance to choose form and timing.

Citation:
- Title: The ergogenic effects of acute carbohydrate feeding on endurance performance: a systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression
- Authors: Domingo Jesus Ramos-Campo and colleagues
- Year: 2024
- Source type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.1080/10408398.2023.2233633
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/10408398.2023.2233633

## Post-exercise fueling matters most when recovery time is short

Evidence summary:
Nutrient timing guidance emphasizes carbohydrate restoration after high-volume or intense exercise, especially when the next session is soon. Combining carbohydrate with protein can support recovery goals when total daily intake and timing are both relevant.

Practical PaceUp use:
After long runs, races, or hard workouts, recommend a familiar meal or snack with carbohydrates and protein. Emphasize this more strongly when the runner has another workout within 24 hours.
