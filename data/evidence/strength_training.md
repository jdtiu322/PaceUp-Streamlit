# Strength Training Evidence

## Strength training can improve running economy and performance markers

Evidence summary:
A systematic review of middle- and long-distance runners found that strength training often improves running economy and some time-trial or sprint performance outcomes. The review did not find consistent negative effects on body composition.

Practical PaceUp use:
Recommend 2 to 3 strength sessions per week for runners when recovery allows. Prioritize consistency and progressive loading over long, exhausting gym sessions.

Citation:
- Title: Effects of Strength Training on the Physiological Determinants of Middle- and Long-Distance Running Performance: A Systematic Review
- Authors: Richard C. Blagrove, Glyn Howatson, Philip R. Hayes
- Year: 2018
- Source type: Systematic review
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-017-0835-7
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29249083/

## Heavy resistance, plyometric, and combined methods can be useful

Evidence summary:
A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis found that strength training can improve running economy, with effects depending on method and speed. High-load and combined methods showed benefits in some speed ranges, and plyometric training showed benefits in lower speed ranges. Certainty varied by method.

Practical PaceUp use:
For healthy runners, combine basic heavy strength, calf and hip work, and low-volume plyometrics after a foundation phase. For beginners or injury-prone runners, start with simple controlled strength before adding jumps.

Citation:
- Title: Effect of Strength Training Programs in Middle- and Long-Distance Runners' Economy at Different Running Speeds: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis
- Authors: Cristian Llanos-Lagos, Rodrigo Ramirez-Campillo, Jason Moran, Eduardo Saez de Villarreal
- Year: 2024
- Source type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-023-01978-y
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38165636/

## Strength work should not replace too much running specificity

Evidence summary:
Strength training can support running economy and performance determinants, but endurance performance still depends heavily on consistent running, aerobic development, and event-specific work. Strength should complement, not crowd out, the key running sessions.

Practical PaceUp use:
Place strength on easy days or after quality sessions when appropriate, leaving enough recovery before hard workouts and long runs. Reduce gym volume during race week or when fatigue is high.

Citation:
- Title: Effects of Strength Training on the Physiological Determinants of Middle- and Long-Distance Running Performance: A Systematic Review
- Authors: Richard C. Blagrove, Glyn Howatson, Philip R. Hayes
- Year: 2018
- Source type: Systematic review
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-017-0835-7
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5889786/

## Strength training supports economy more than VO2max

Evidence summary:
An umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses reported moderate to large effects on running economy across included reviews, while VO2max generally did not show meaningful change. The authors also noted limitations in review quality.

Practical PaceUp use:
Explain strength work as a durability and economy tool, not mainly a VO2max tool. It can help the runner use energy more efficiently and tolerate training better.

Citation:
- Title: The Effect of Strength Training on Endurance Performance Determinants in Middle- and Long-Distance Endurance Athletes: An Umbrella Review of Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis
- Authors: Domingo J. Ramos-Campo, Luis Andreu-Caravaca, Vicente J. Clemente-Suarez, Jacobo A. Rubio-Arias
- Year: 2025
- Source type: Umbrella review
- DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000005056
- PMID: 40153564
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40153564/
