# Recovery And Sleep Evidence

## Sleep extension and naps are promising athlete sleep interventions

Evidence summary:
A systematic review of sleep interventions in competitive athletes found that sleep extension had the most consistent beneficial effects on subsequent performance among the intervention types reviewed. The evidence base was still limited, with small samples and varied sports.

Practical PaceUp use:
When a runner is tired or underperforming, suggest increasing sleep opportunity before adding training. Short naps can be useful when nighttime sleep is restricted, but they should not replace adequate regular sleep.

Citation:
- Title: Sleep Interventions Designed to Improve Athletic Performance and Recovery: A Systematic Review of Current Approaches
- Authors: Daniel Bonnar, Kate Bartel, Naomi Kakoschke, Christin Lang
- Year: 2018
- Source type: Systematic review
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-017-0832-x
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29352373/

## Athletes often sleep less than general recommendations

Evidence summary:
A systematic review using objective sleep measures found that athletes averaged less than 8 hours of sleep per night across included datasets. Sleep duration and efficiency varied by age, sport, and training period, with heavy training periods associated with shorter sleep and lower sleep efficiency.

Practical PaceUp use:
Ask about sleep when runners report fatigue, irritability, soreness, or stalled progress. Treat poor sleep as a training variable, not just a lifestyle detail.

Citation:
- Title: Deconstructing athletes' sleep: A systematic review of the influence of age, sex, athletic expertise, sport type, and season on sleep characteristics
- Authors: Angelos Vlahoyiannis, George Aphamis, Gregory C. Bogdanis, Giorgos K. Sakkas, Eleni Andreou, Christoforos D. Giannaki
- Year: 2021
- Source type: Systematic review
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jshs.2020.03.006
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32325024/

## Sleep loss can affect sport-specific and cognitive performance

Evidence summary:
A review on sleep loss and athletic performance found mixed effects across physical tasks, but noted concerns for sport-specific performance, cognitive performance, mood, immune function, and physiological stress responses.

Practical PaceUp use:
If a runner slept poorly before a hard workout, suggest reducing intensity, shifting the workout, or using effort-based targets. Poor sleep is a valid reason to adjust training.

Citation:
- Title: Sleep and athletic performance: the effects of sleep loss on exercise performance, and physiological and cognitive responses to exercise
- Authors: Hugh H. K. Fullagar, Sabrina Skorski, Rob Duffield, Daniel Hammes, Aaron J. Coutts, Tim Meyer
- Year: 2015
- Source type: Review
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-014-0260-0
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25315456/

## Sleep interventions may help performance, but claims should stay measured

Evidence summary:
A later systematic review of athlete sleep interventions found that sleep extension and naps were among the most representative and effective strategies for improving sleep and performance outcomes. The authors still emphasized limitations in study quality and the need for more research.

Practical PaceUp use:
Recommend realistic sleep extension and naps as recovery tools, but avoid guaranteeing performance gains. Use sleep guidance especially during heavy training blocks, taper weeks, travel, and race week.

Citation:
- Title: The Impact of Sleep Interventions on Athletic Performance: A Systematic Review
- Authors: Lucio A. Cunha, Julio A. Costa, Elisa A. Marques, Joao Brito, Michele Lastella, Pedro Figueiredo
- Year: 2023
- Source type: Systematic review
- DOI: 10.1186/s40798-023-00599-z
- PMID: 37462808
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10354314/
