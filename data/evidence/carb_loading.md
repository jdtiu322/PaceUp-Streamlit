# Carbohydrate Loading Evidence

## A high carbohydrate diet over several days can substantially increase muscle glycogen

Evidence summary:
Systematic review and meta-analytic evidence shows that glycogen supercompensation occurs after exhaustive exercise followed by several days of high carbohydrate intake. This supports carbohydrate loading as a real physiological strategy for long endurance events rather than a myth or purely psychological ritual.

Practical PaceUp use:
For half-marathon and marathon athletes, PaceUp can explain that carb loading is mainly about topping off glycogen before long races. Keep the guidance practical: increase carbohydrate intake for several days before the event, reduce unnecessary training stress, and avoid extreme last-minute experimentation.

Citation:
- Title: Glycogen supercompensation in skeletal muscle after cycling or running followed by a high carbohydrate intake the following days: a systematic review and meta-analysis
- Authors: Kristian Solem, Matthieu Clauss, Jørgen Jensen
- Year: 2025
- Source type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.3389/fphys.2025.1620943
- PMID: 40901614
- URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2025.1620943

## A simpler multi-day high carbohydrate strategy can work without an elaborate depletion protocol

Evidence summary:
Experimental evidence suggests that repeated glycogen supercompensation can occur with a four-day high carbohydrate feeding strategy after exhaustive exercise. This supports simpler modern loading approaches rather than older, more punitive depletion regimens.

Practical PaceUp use:
Avoid recommending a harsh depletion week. A cleaner message is usually better: reduce training, keep meals familiar, and increase carbohydrate intake enough to support glycogen storage without gastrointestinal chaos.

Citation:
- Title: Repeated muscle glycogen supercompensation with four days' recovery between exhaustive exercise
- Authors: Thomas M. Doering, Gregory R. Cox, José L. Areta, Vernon G. Coffey
- Year: 2019
- Source type: Experimental study
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jsams.2019.03.009
- PMID: 30940441
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2019.03.009
