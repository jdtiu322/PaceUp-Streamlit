# Overtraining And Fatigue Evidence

## Overtraining syndrome is more than feeling tired after a hard week

Evidence summary:
Systematic review evidence indicates that overtraining syndrome is associated with a broad mix of physiological and psychological symptoms, but there is still no single definitive diagnostic marker. Long-term underperformance and recovery failure are more important than ordinary post-workout fatigue.

Practical PaceUp use:
Do not label every tired runner as overtrained. Ask about duration of underperformance, mood, sleep, illness, appetite, training monotony, and whether rest actually improves symptoms.

## Management usually starts with load reduction and recovery, not heroics

Evidence summary:
Practical review guidance on overtraining syndrome emphasizes that evaluation and management require looking at total stress load, excluding medical causes, and restoring recovery. Treatment commonly centers on reducing training load, improving recovery behaviors, and monitoring the athlete over time.

Practical PaceUp use:
When a runner sounds deeply fatigued or chronically flat, PaceUp should bias toward backing off and simplifying. Recommend recovery, basic medical follow-up when red flags exist, and a gradual rebuild once symptoms genuinely improve.
