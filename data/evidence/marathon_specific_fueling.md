# Marathon Specific Fueling Evidence

## Marathon fueling usually requires carbohydrate during the race, not just before it

Evidence summary:
Review evidence focused on endurance running supports carbohydrate ingestion during prolonged running events as a meaningful performance aid. Marathon runners should not rely only on breakfast or carb loading if they expect to be on course for a substantial duration.

Practical PaceUp use:
For marathon advice, PaceUp should treat during-race fueling as part of pacing strategy, not an optional extra. Encourage runners to practice a schedule in long runs and adjust it for pace, weather, and stomach tolerance.

Citation:
- Title: Does Carbohydrate Intake During Endurance Running Improve Performance? A Critical Review
- Authors: Patrick B. Wilson
- Year: 2016
- Source type: Critical review
- DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001430
- PMID: 27045602
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27045602/

## Very long events can benefit from higher carbohydrate delivery using multiple transportable carbohydrates

Evidence summary:
Review guidance indicates that endurance athletes competing beyond roughly 2.5 hours can oxidize higher carbohydrate amounts when glucose or maltodextrin is combined with fructose. This is one reason marathon and ultra fueling plans often differ from shorter-race guidance.

Practical PaceUp use:
For slower marathoners or highly competitive runners aiming to fuel aggressively, suggest testing higher carbohydrate rates with mixed carbohydrate sources. Keep the emphasis on practice and gut tolerance rather than forcing a number on race day.

Citation:
- Title: The use of carbohydrates during exercise as an ergogenic aid
- Authors: Asker E. Jeukendrup
- Year: 2013
- Source type: Review
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-013-0079-0
- PMID: 23846824
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23846824/

## Multiple transportable carbohydrate blends can outperform glucose-only strategies at high intake rates

Evidence summary:
Controlled performance evidence suggests that mixed glucose plus fructose formulations can support better long-distance endurance performance than glucose-only strategies when intake rates are high.

Practical PaceUp use:
When helping a runner choose gels or drinks for a marathon, PaceUp can explain why product formulation matters. This supports planning with specific brands or mixes in training rather than selecting gels randomly at the expo.

Citation:
- Title: Superior endurance performance with ingestion of multiple transportable carbohydrates
- Authors: Kevin Currell, Asker E. Jeukendrup
- Year: 2008
- Source type: Controlled performance study
- DOI: 10.1249/mss.0b013e31815adf19
- PMID: 18202575
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18202575/
