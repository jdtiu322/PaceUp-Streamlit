# Gut Training Evidence

## Repeated feeding exposure can improve gastrointestinal tolerance during endurance exercise

Evidence summary:
Systematic review evidence suggests that repeated exposure to carbohydrate feeding before and during endurance exercise can improve markers of gastrointestinal tolerance and may reduce some exercise-associated gut symptoms in trained athletes.

Practical PaceUp use:
When runners struggle with gels or sports drink, avoid telling them they simply "cannot fuel." Instead, frame tolerance as trainable and recommend practicing small, repeatable doses during long runs and race-specific sessions.

Citation:
- Title: The Effect of Gut-Training and Feeding-Challenge on Markers of Gastrointestinal Status in Response to Endurance Exercise: A Systematic Literature Review
- Authors: Isabel G. Martinez, Alice S. Mika, Jessica R. Biesiekierski, Ricardo J. S. Costa
- Year: 2023
- Source type: Systematic literature review
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-023-01841-0
- PMID: 37061651
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37061651/

## High carbohydrate race practice may help tolerance more than performance in the short term

Evidence summary:
A short-term intervention in highly trained endurance athletes found that very high carbohydrate intake and gut-training had modest effects on gastrointestinal status and only minor effects on performance over the study period. This supports using gut training primarily to improve tolerance and execution rather than promising dramatic speed gains by itself.

Practical PaceUp use:
Tell runners to separate two goals: learning to tolerate race fueling and trying to improve performance. Early gut training should prioritize consistency, comfort, and finishing long runs without stomach blowups.

Citation:
- Title: Short-Term Very High Carbohydrate Diet and Gut-Training Have Minor Effects on Gastrointestinal Status and Performance in Highly Trained Endurance Athletes
- Authors: Andy J. King, Naroa Etxebarria, Megan L. Ross, Laura Garvican-Lewis, Louise M. Burke, and colleagues
- Year: 2022
- Source type: Clinical trial
- DOI: 10.3390/nu14091929
- PMID: 35565896
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35565896/
