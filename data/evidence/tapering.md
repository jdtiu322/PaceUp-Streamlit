# Tapering Evidence

## Reducing volume while keeping intensity can improve race readiness

Evidence summary:
A meta-analysis of competitive athletes found that tapering can improve performance when training volume is reduced while intensity and frequency are mostly maintained. The clearest signal favored a taper of about 2 weeks with a substantial volume reduction.

Practical PaceUp use:
For goal races, recommend reducing total workload during the final 1 to 2 weeks while keeping a small amount of controlled faster running. Avoid removing all intensity unless fatigue, injury, or illness requires it.

Citation:
- Title: Effects of tapering on performance: a meta-analysis
- Authors: Laurent Bosquet, Jonathan Montpetit, Denis Arvisais, Inigo Mujika
- Year: 2007
- Source type: Meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.1249/mss.0b013e31806010e0
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/

## Tapering should reduce fatigue without losing fitness

Evidence summary:
A review by Mujika and Padilla described tapering as a planned reduction in training load before competition. The aim is to lower accumulated fatigue while preserving training adaptations. Their review supports maintaining intensity, reducing volume, and avoiding a large drop in training frequency for most competitive athletes.

Practical PaceUp use:
When a runner asks how to taper, explain that the goal is to feel fresh but still sharp. Replace long or hard sessions with shorter versions rather than stopping structured running entirely.

Citation:
- Title: Scientific Bases for Precompetition Tapering Strategies
- Authors: Inigo Mujika, Sabino Padilla
- Year: 2003
- Source type: Review
- DOI: 10.1249/01.MSS.0000074448.73931.11
- URL: https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/Fulltext/2003/07000/Scientific_Bases_for_Precompetition_Tapering.17.aspx

## Taper advice should scale with race distance and runner experience

Evidence summary:
Taper studies include competitive athletes across endurance sports, so exact taper prescriptions should not be applied rigidly to every recreational runner. The evidence supports the principle of reduced load, but the exact duration and amount of reduction should depend on race distance, recent fatigue, training age, and injury status.

Practical PaceUp use:
For a 5K or 10K, a shorter taper may be enough. For a half marathon or marathon, use a longer reduction in volume, especially if the runner has high mileage or signs of fatigue.

- Title: Effects of tapering on performance: a meta-analysis
