# Return To Running Evidence

## Return timelines after bone stress injuries depend heavily on injury site and severity

Evidence summary:
Systematic review and meta-analytic evidence shows that return-to-sport timelines after bone stress injuries vary substantially by anatomical site, with high-risk locations taking longer and carrying greater complication risk than lower-risk sites.

Practical PaceUp use:
If a runner is returning from a suspected or confirmed bone stress injury, avoid generic timelines. Encourage clinician-guided clearance for high-risk sites and use very conservative expectations for return when pain history or imaging suggests a more serious lesion.

Citation:
- Title: Return to sport following low-risk and high-risk bone stress injuries: a systematic review and meta-analysis
- Authors: Tim Hoenig, Julian Eissele, André Strahl, Kristin L. Popp, Adam S. Tenforde, and colleagues
- Year: 2023
- Source type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.1136/bjsports-2022-106328
- PMID: 36720584
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36720584/

## Imaging grade can help explain why some runners need a slower comeback

Evidence summary:
Meta-analytic evidence indicates that higher MRI grading in bone stress injuries is associated with longer return-to-sport timelines. This supports individualized rehabilitation decisions rather than assuming every runner can resume on the same schedule.

Practical PaceUp use:
When a runner asks for a comeback plan after a stress reaction or stress fracture, frame the answer around symptom response and medical context. If MRI grade or injury site is known, use that to justify caution instead of forcing a rigid calendar.

Citation:
- Title: Does Magnetic Resonance Imaging Grading Correlate With Return to Sports After Bone Stress Injuries? A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis
- Authors: Tim Hoenig, Adam S. Tenforde, André Strahl, Tim Rolvien, Karsten Hollander
- Year: 2022
- Source type: Systematic review and meta-analysis
- DOI: 10.1177/0363546521993807
- PMID: 33720786
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33720786/
